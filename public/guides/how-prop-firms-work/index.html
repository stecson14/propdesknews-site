How prop firms really work: straight answers

Most prop firm accounts, funded ones included, are simulated: you trade a demo account and the firm pays you a share of the simulated profit from its own money. A few firms move steady traders to a live account later. Here is how that works, firm by firm, from their own rules.

Edited by Steven T. · Sources listed at the end

The short version

  • Most funded accounts are simulated, and the firms say so. The payout is real money from the firm's own funds.
  • A few firms move steady traders to a live account, with different rules.
  • Topstep's privacy policy lets partners use deidentified trade data, including mirroring it in real markets.

Every answer here is quoted from the firm's own help center, terms or privacy policy, read between Nov 3 and Nov 11, 2026. Rules change often; the firm's own page is the final word.

Are funded accounts just demo accounts?

At most firms, yes. The account you trade after passing is a simulated account on live market data, and the firm pays you a share of the simulated profit from its own money. New to all this?

Start with what a prop firm is.

The firms say it plainly. FTMO: "all accounts we provide to our clients are demo accounts with fictitious funds and any trading is in a simulated environment only." MyFundedFutures: its programs are "conducted entirely in a simulated, non-executing environment." Topstep's Express Funded Account is simulated; its Live Funded Account is not: "Is the live trading capital my money? No. It's Topstep's prop firm capital."

Do prop firms give you real money?

Yes, the payout is real money. The firm sends it from its own funds, by bank transfer or a payout service, even though the account that earned it is simulated. Topstep: "Topstep pays you real money based on your simulated trading results."

The firms publish how much they have paid: Topstep's homepage says $0.7B+, FTMO's footer says $650M+ and Tradeify says over $350 million (read Nov 6, 2026). Those are the firms' own claims; compare the payouts firms report.

A total shows a firm has paid, not that it always will: the graveyard lists firms that stopped.

Do prop firms want you to lose?

Their income doesn't come from your losing trades; it comes from fees, and most fees come from traders who don't pass. Topstep says fees come from every trader who buys an evaluation, whether or not they pass, and in 2025 16.8% of its Trading Combines were passed and 33.3% of funded traders got a payout.

Payouts are the firm's biggest cost, but paid traders keep buying and tell others, which is why firms that pay publish their totals; the full picture is in how prop firms make money.

Do prop firms copy your trades or steal your strategy?

Some firms say they may use your trades, as data. Topstep's privacy policy says it collects data on your simulated trades and patterns and may share it, aggregated and deidentified, with partners who "may use all such information to influence or inform their own trading activities, for example, through mirroring simulated trades (or entering trades opposite of simulated trades) in actual financial markets."

So "copying" there means trading real markets on what simulated traders do as a group. None of the firm pages we read say a firm copies one trader's strategy, and we won't guess beyond their words. Read the privacy policy before you buy.

Do prop firms share data with each other?

The privacy policies we read don't name other prop firms. Topstep's lists "licensed brokerages and other financial industry vendors", its affiliates, payment, fraud prevention and analytics providers, advertising partners, and "Third Party Businesses".

What firms do share is plumbing: many run on Tradovate or Rithmic, and ID checks come from vendors such as Sumsub (Tradeify) and Veriff (Alpha Futures). When each firm checks your ID: KYC rules at every firm.

What broker do prop firms use?

For simulated accounts, none in your name. You get a platform login and a data feed. MyFundedFutures says it "is not a broker-dealer, investment adviser, introducing broker, futures commission merchant (FCM), or money manager," and FTMO says it does "not act as a broker."

Do prop firms charge commission?

Yes, per contract or per lot, even on simulated accounts, and it comes out of your profit.

Do prop firms have slippage?

Yes, on simulated and live accounts alike. Simulated fills come from the firm's fill algorithm, not a real order book, so firms ban strategies built to exploit it. Topstep: "Slippage occurs in both simulated and live trading across all financial markets."

MyFundedFutures explains the gap: "Certain trading strategies may exploit the simulated fill algorithm, performing well in the evaluation stage but inevitably resulting in losses when transitioned to live markets." Live accounts plan for it: TradeDay keeps a slippage buffer on Funded Live ($500 in its 50K example). To see what one tick of slippage costs, check the MNQ tick value.

Why do prop firms move you to live?

A live account puts the firm's real capital at risk, so firms keep it for traders who have already been paid. Each firm sets its own trigger.

  • Topstep: not automatic. "Traders with Topstep average over $40k in lifetime payouts before being called up," and "100% of traders called to our Live program have taken a payout." Live payouts have no cap and turn daily after 30 winning days of $150 or more.
  • MyFundedFutures: Rapid moves live automatically after a $10,000 day; Builder after up to 5 sim payouts.
  • Tradeify: considered after 3 payouts on one account or 10 in total.
  • TradeDay: Funded Live pays 90/10 but starts at a $0 balance with a new drawdown.
  • Earn2Trade: Live accounts use a real-time trailing drawdown instead of end of day.

What changes: the drawdown, payout timing and real costs (Topstep passes on CME professional data at $133 per exchange a month). See how a new drawdown moves in the Drawdown Lab.

Why do futures prop firms charge monthly?

Because many futures evaluations are subscriptions: you pay each month until you pass or cancel, which keeps the first price low. Topstep's Standard Path costs $49, $99 or $199 a month for a 50K, 100K or 150K Trading Combine. Others charge once: LucidFlex is a one-time fee with no monthly rebill.

Futures accounts also look cheaper than forex and CFD challenges because they allow smaller losses. A Topstep 50K fails at a $2,000 loss; an FTMO 100K 2-Step can lose $10,000. Compare futures firms with no monthly fee and the cheapest futures prop firms.

Do prop firms have a time limit?

Most futures firms have no deadline to pass now, but the subscription keeps billing while you try. Elite Trader Funding: no time limit, evaluations bill every 30 days and your progress carries over at renewal. Apex's EOD evaluation is an exception, with 30 days to hit the target.

CFD firms vary by program. FXIFY lists no time limit on its evaluations, except Lightning, which has a 7-day window. How long passing really takes: how long to pass a prop firm.

How do prop firms detect copy trading?

They see your IP, device, card and every fill. Apex: "Sharing MAC addresses, computers, IPs, credit cards, or trade copying with other traders is strictly forbidden." Topstep bans coordinated trading, meaning trades placed in concert with others to "trade the same or opposite strategy simultaneously," and says "VPNs, proxy services, TOR, geo-location obfuscation, and other identity-masking services are not permitted."

Copying your own accounts is a separate rule: Apex requires all your Performance Accounts to trade in the same direction. Limits per firm: how many accounts you can have, and firms that allow automated trading.

Do prop firms let you trade options?

Mostly no. Futures firms publish the products you can trade, and the lists are futures contracts. Elite Trader Funding says U.S. futures only, with no options, including options on futures.

Topstep lists CME Group futures only, and TradeDay lists CME Group futures on CME, CBOT, COMEX and NYMEX only. Tick values for each: futures contract specs.

Questions

Is a funded account real money?

At most firms the funded account is simulated, but the payout is real money from the firm's own funds. A few firms, such as Topstep and TradeDay, later move some traders to a live account.

Do prop firms trade against you?

On a simulated account there is no real trade to take the other side of. Topstep's privacy policy says partners may use deidentified data from simulated trades, including by entering trades opposite of them in real markets.

Do prop firms see my strategy?

Yes, every trade, because they run the account. Topstep's privacy policy says it may share that data, aggregated and deidentified, with partners.

Do futures prop firms use a broker?

Not for simulated accounts. You trade through a platform and data feed such as TopstepX, Tradovate or Rithmic. Live accounts send real orders to the market.

Can a prop firm move me to a live account without asking?

At some firms, yes: MyFundedFutures moves Rapid accounts live automatically after a $10,000 day. Bulenox asks you to accept its Funded Account Agreement, and declining closes that Master account.
