How prop firms make money

A prop firm earns in two ways: fees from every trader who buys an evaluation, and a cut of the profits it pays out to funded traders. Knowing how the money moves tells you what you'll really spend and what to read before you buy.

By Prop Desk Staff · Sources listed at the end

The short version

  • Topstep explains it in its own guide: evaluation-based firms "make money from evaluation fees and from their share of trader profits." Fees come from every buyer, pass or fail.
  • Fees can include a monthly subscription, a one-time evaluation fee, an activation fee when you pass, resets, and market data. Not every firm charges every one.
  • On the payout side, the firm keeps a share of each payout, for example 10% when a trader is on Topstep's or Bulenox's 90% split.
  • Topstep's 2025 numbers: 16.8% of Combines passed, but 51.8% of people passed at least once, so many people bought more than one. One in three funded traders got a payout.
  • Firms publish payout totals ($0.7B+ at Topstep, $350M+ at Tradeify, $300M+ at MFFU) and, more usefully, the share of funded traders who got paid: 33.3%, 28.5% and 39.2%.
  • Payout rules decide how much of a funded account's profit you can actually take out. Read them as closely as the price.

Every fee and number here is quoted from the firm's or the CFTC's own page, read on the date shown. Prices change often; the firm's checkout is the final word.

The two money streams

Topstep's beginner guide puts it in three sentences: "Evaluation-based prop firms make money from evaluation fees and from their share of trader profits. Fees come from every trader who buys an evaluation, whether or not they pass. The profit split applies only when a funded trader has profits and requests a payout."

On a simulated account, the payout is the firm's own money. Topstep's help center: "Topstep pays you real money based on your simulated trading results." So the firm's income is the fees it collects, and its biggest cost is the payouts it sends.

Two fine-print points from Topstep's pricing page. A promo code on a subscription covers the "First month only" unless stated otherwise, and "Most fees at Topstep are non-refundable." Check both at any firm before you count on a discount or a refund.

What Topstep's 2025 numbers show

Topstep publishes its 2025 results on its site. From January to December 2025:

  • 16.8% of all Trading Combines started were passed.
  • 51.8% of people who entered one or more Combines passed at least one.
  • 33.3% of people at the Funded Level got a payout.
  • 0.71% of people in an Express Funded Account were called up to a Live Funded Account.

The gap between the first two lines is the key one. If about 1 in 6 Combines is passed but about half of people pass at least once, many people bought several Combines before passing. Each one is a fee, while payouts go to the third of funded traders who get paid.

Topstep says these statistics cover both simulated and live trading and are not indicative of future results.

Do prop firms actually pay out?

Yes, and the useful question is how often. Two kinds of number answer it. Firms publish a running total of what they have paid, and a few publish the share of funded traders who ever received a payout. The totals are each firm's own claim, which we can't audit; the percentages are the stronger signal, and they come with the firm's own disclaimer that they are not indicative of future results.

Put the two together and a payout is the exception, not the norm: roughly one funded trader in three at the firms that publish the figure, and getting funded is itself the hard part. Why payout rules matter as much as price

Payout rules decide how much of a funded account's profit becomes money in your bank, and how fast. The common ones:

  • Caps per payout. LucidFlex caps each payout at $2,000 on a 50K, for example.
  • Consistency rules. Most firms on our rules pages limit how much of your profit can come from one day. Our rules pages show each firm's version and include a calculator.
  • Minimum days and buffers. Some firms require a number of profitable days, or a cushion above your drawdown, before the first payout.
  • The split itself. What share of each payout you keep.

Whatever the reason a firm gives for a rule, its effect is on the timing and size of payouts. Two firms with the same price can pay out very differently.

When the money doesn't add up

The CFTC's 2023 complaint against My Forex Funds shows what happens when the two sides of the ledger are the whole business. It alleged the firm took in about $310 million in fees from more than 135,000 customers and paid out about $137 million, and that "When customers make money, it means that Traders Global loses money."

Those were allegations, and in 2025 the CFTC's own conduct in the case drew court sanctions. The case is covered in our guide on how to check a prop firm.

Sales, codes and affiliates

Firms also spend to find buyers: sales, discount codes and affiliate programs that pay sites and creators for sign-ups. Prop Desk is an affiliate of some firms. We label every affiliate code and link, and our affiliate disclosure explains how it works. Every live public and affiliate code is on the deals page.

What it means for you

  1. Count the full cost. Evaluation fee or months of subscription, plus activation, resets and data. Assume more than one attempt.
  2. Read the payout rules before the price. Caps, consistency and buffers decide what you can take out.
  3. Treat the fee as spent. It's paid whether or not you pass, and at Topstep, for one, most fees are non-refundable.
  4. Check the firm's track record. Our news log records every confirmed rule change and closure.

Questions

How do prop firms make money?

From evaluation fees and from a share of the profits they pay out. In Topstep's words, fees come from every trader who buys an evaluation, whether or not they pass, and the profit split applies only when a funded trader has profits and requests a payout.

Do prop firms actually pay out?

Yes. Topstep says it has paid out $0.7B+ all time and that 33.3% of funded traders got a payout in 2025; Tradeify reports $350M+ and 28.5% of funded traders paid (Aug 2025 to Jul 2026); MyFundedFutures reports $300M+ and 39.2%. The totals are the firms' own claims; the percentages show that roughly one funded trader in three gets paid.

Where does payout money come from on a simulated account?

From the firm. Topstep says it pays real money based on your simulated trading results. The CFTC's complaint against My Forex Funds described the same thing for that firm: customer profits came out of the firm's own pocket.

What share of profits do prop firms keep?

It depends on the firm and the account. Topstep says traders keep up to 90%. Bulenox says the trader receives 90% of each additional payout and it takes a 10% commission when the payout is sent.

Are prop firm fees refundable?

Usually not. Topstep's pricing page says most fees at Topstep are non-refundable. Check the refund terms at any firm before you buy.

Does a promo code discount every month of a subscription?

Not always. Topstep's pricing page says the first month only is billed at the promo price unless stated otherwise. Read the code's terms on the firm's checkout.

How many traders pass a prop firm evaluation?

Topstep publishes its figures: in 2025, 16.8% of Trading Combines started were passed, 51.8% of people passed at least one, and 33.3% of people at the Funded Level got a payout.
