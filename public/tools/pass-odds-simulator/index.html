How the simulator works

Each run starts a fresh evaluation at $0 and plays your trades one at a time: a win adds your average win, a loss takes away your average loss, with your win rate deciding which. A run passes the moment profit reaches the target, blows the account the moment it touches the drawdown line, and is still going if neither happens in the days you give it. The tool plays 1,000 runs and counts them.

The drawdown line moves the way the type says. Static sits at minus the max drawdown and never moves. End-of-day trailing moves up to your best closing balance minus the drawdown, once a day. Intraday trailing moves up with every new high, after each trade. Both trailing lines stop at the starting balance, the way most futures firms write it. The Drawdown Lab draws the three lines on one equity path if you want to see the difference.

The same seed is used every time, so the same inputs always give the same answer, and a link you share shows the same result.

How to read your result

Average trade first. Win rate times average win, minus loss rate times average loss, is what one trade is worth on average. Above zero, time helps you, and a bigger drawdown or more days raises the pass rate. At or below zero, more days mostly mean more blown accounts.

Tries per pass. If 25% of runs pass, you should expect about four evaluations, resets included, for each pass. Multiply by the fee to see what a pass really costs; our True Cost Index has each firm's list price.

Trades a day is risk. More trades a day with the same edge reach the target sooner but also swing harder, so the drawdown gets more chances. A daily loss limit cuts the bad days short, which usually lowers the blow-up rate.

It is your numbers, not a firm's pass rate. Firms publish few pass rates, and ours are in the guides only where a firm printed one. This tool answers a different question: given how you trade, how often would this plan work?

Questions

What are my chances of passing a prop firm evaluation?

It depends on your edge and the plan. Put in your real win rate, average win and average loss from your journal, then the firm's target and drawdown, and the simulator plays 1,000 evaluations to show how often that combination passes, blows the account or runs out of days.

Does a trailing drawdown lower the pass rate?

Usually, yes. A trailing line follows your best balance up, so a winner you give back costs you room, and intraday trailing does it on every new high. Run the same numbers with Static, End-of-day and Intraday to see how much it changes for you.

How many trades a day should I take in an evaluation?

With a positive average trade, more trades reach the target faster but also give the drawdown more chances to hit. Try your numbers at 2, 4 and 8 trades a day and compare the blown-account share, not just the pass rate.

Is this a firm's official pass rate?

No. It is a simulation of the numbers you enter, with the same seed each time. It leaves out streaks, slippage, fees and rule details like consistency or minimum days, so treat it as a way to compare plans, not a forecast.
