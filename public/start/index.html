How a prop firm eval works

A prop firm sells you a test. Pass it and they give you a simulated account and pay you a share of what you make on it. Five steps from "which firm" to "first payout," with the fine print that matters at each one.

1 · Pick2 · Pay3 · Pass4 · Activate5 · Get paidChecklist
1

Pick a market and a firm

Futures firms (ES, NQ, CL, GC) hand you a platform login and sim account. CFD and forex firms run on MT5, cTrader or a browser platform. Perps firms trade crypto perpetuals 24/7. Each market has its own firms, rules and price range.

  • Shortlist two or three firms, not ten. The directory filters by market and shows whether a sale is on.
  • Decide on the drawdown type before the price. An end-of-day trailing drawdown and an intraday trailing drawdown are different products at the same price. See drawdown types.
  • Check the platform list. If you can't run NinjaTrader, a firm that only offers NinjaTrader is a bad fit whatever the discount.

2

Know the true cost before you pay

The eval fee is the smallest number. What you actually spend is the eval, plus a reset or two if you fail, plus an activation fee when you pass, plus renewals if the eval is billed monthly and you take longer than a month.

  • One-time evals stay open until you pass or fail. Monthly evals renew; cancel them when you're done.
  • Discount codes usually apply to the eval only, not to resets or activation. Our live deals say what each code skips.
  • Buying five accounts to "diversify" multiplies every fee by five. Start with one.
Do thisRun the true cost calculator with one reset and the activation fee. That number is what you're risking.
3

Pass the evaluation

You need to hit the profit target without touching the drawdown line, breaking a daily loss limit, or tripping a consistency rule. Most firms also require a minimum number of trading days.

  • Size for the drawdown, not the target. A $2,000 drawdown with $400 average losing days gives you five bad days. The runway calculator does this math.
  • Trailing drawdowns move up with your open profit at some firms. A winner that comes back to break-even can still cost you drawdown room.
  • News releases are where evals die. Know the week's red folders and the firm's news-trading rule before the session.

4

Activate the funded account

Passing gets you a funded account, which at most futures firms is still simulated (a "performance" or "sim-funded" account). The firm pays you from its own revenue. You usually pay an activation fee, one-time or monthly, to open it.

  • The rules change here. Read the funded-stage drawdown, consistency rule, contract scaling and payout rules separately from the eval rules.
  • Some firms move you to a live account after a run of payouts; that stage usually has a different profit split.
  • Expect identity verification before activation or the first payout. It's normal; have your documents ready.
Do thisOpen the firm's profile and read the "rules" card again as a funded trader, not as an eval taker.
5

Get paid

Payout rules decide when money moves: a minimum amount, a number of qualifying days, a payout cycle, sometimes a buffer you must keep in the account, and the profit split.

  • Count the days before you buy. Five qualifying days at a $150 minimum plus a $500 minimum payout is a very different product from "withdraw from day one."
  • A consistency rule can hold a payout even when the balance qualifies. Spread your profit across days as you get close.
  • Payouts are usually paid to you as a contractor. Keep records for tax time.

What ends most accounts

  • Doubling size after a loss to "get it back." The drawdown doesn't care that you were right the next day.
  • Holding through a red-folder release with a trailing drawdown and a stop that fills late.
  • One huge day that breaks the consistency rule a week before payout.
  • Forgetting a monthly renewal on an account you stopped trading.
  • Buying more accounts than you can watch, then copying one mistake onto all of them.
